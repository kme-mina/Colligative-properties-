Effect of Solute Concentration on Colligative Properties

Change the solute concentration and watch how all four properties change together.

Key idea: Nonvolatile solute particles occupy part of the liquid surface, so fewer solvent molecules are able to evaporate. As a result, the vapor above the solution reaches equilibrium at a lower pressure than the vapor above the pure solvent.

Key idea: A liquid boils when its vapor pressure reaches atmospheric pressure (760 mmHg). The vapor pressure of the solution is lower, so it must be heated to a higher temperature to boil.

Key idea: A semipermeable membrane lets only water molecules pass through. Water crosses in both directions, but more crosses toward the solution; this net movement is called osmosis. The pressure needed to stop it is the osmotic pressure.

Application: the red blood cell

Summary

  • Colligative properties depend on the number of solute particles (the concentration), not on the identity of the solute.
  • Increasing the concentration leads to a larger vapor-pressure lowering, a larger boiling-point elevation, a larger freezing-point depression, and a higher osmotic pressure.
  • An ionic solute produces more particles (NaCl: 2 particles, CaCl₂: 3 particles), so its effect is greater than that of a molecular solute at the same concentration.
The solute is assumed to be nonvolatile and the solution dilute. The accuracy of the results decreases at high concentrations.
